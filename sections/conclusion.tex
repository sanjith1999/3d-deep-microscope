\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The optics decide how much depth the measurements contain; the prior can only use it. A phase plate learned once, with a specimen-free depth-decorrelation objective and no decoder in the loop, lowered the depth error of a regulariser, two denoisers and a diffusion posterior, matched a full scan with one eighth of the frames and tightened distribution-free depth intervals, while an in-focus encoder left every decoder depth-blind. Learned optics should be judged by their benefit to any decoder, not only to the one they were trained with.
