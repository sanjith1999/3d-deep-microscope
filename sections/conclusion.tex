\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a decoder-free approach to learning depth-encoding illumination for fixed-focus three-dimensional fluorescence microscopy. By optimising depth discrimination directly through the optical forward model, we learn a phase plate without specimen examples or a reconstruction solver. In simulations, the learned plate reduces depth error by 30--54\,\% across total variation and two plug-and-play reconstruction methods, while narrowing calibrated depth intervals by 27\,\%. The tested in-focus DMD baseline, by contrast, leaves substantial depth ambiguity despite stronger reconstruction priors. These results demonstrate that improving optical measurements can benefit different reconstruction methods without tailoring the encoder to a particular decoder.
