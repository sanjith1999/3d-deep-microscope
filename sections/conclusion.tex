\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Scanning only the input angle of a plane wave through one fixed, defocused phase plate gives a fixed-focus fluorescence microscope depth-distinguishing measurements, because the excitation shears with tilt. With an exactly voxel-integrated operator and standard solvers the volume is recovered without axial scanning, also from eight times fewer measurements than voxels; at equal dose the plate clearly beats in-focus amplitude patterns and a plain angle scan, and modestly a defocused amplitude mask. Many reconstructed connections, however, are not determined by the data at the operating noise level.
