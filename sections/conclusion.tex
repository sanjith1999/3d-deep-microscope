\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We learn a single phase profile for fixed-focus, angle-controlled illumination from the optical model alone, without specimen examples or reconstruction feedback during optical training. In neuronal simulations at matched image count and nominal dose, our plate lowers mean W1 relative to untrained coding and the two tested model-only designs across four tested reconstruction methods. W1 and centroid error measure errors along depth, revealing gains in axial fidelity that similar PSNR between learned plates can conceal. Under iterative reconstruction, our plate's mean W1 is close to the end-to-end plate's, while end-to-end remains better with its own decoder. Crossing separation improves over untrained coding under two of three iterative methods. Together, these results support learning an optical encoder from the model alone and evaluating depth profiles and filament separation beyond intensity similarity.
