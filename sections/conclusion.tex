\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In a computational microscope the optics decide which volumes the measurements allow; the prior only chooses one. On an angle-scanned phase encoder for 3D fluorescence, a plate trained with a data-free depth-decorrelation loss enlarges the set of connections the data certify, a learned prior improves the chosen volume but not what is certified, and photons, not angles, decide the rest. Learned optics should be judged by what their measurements determine, not only by how good the reconstruction looks.
