\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We learn a single phase profile for fixed-focus, angle-controlled illumination from the optical model alone, without specimen examples or reconstruction feedback during optical training. In simulations at eightfold measurement compression and matched nominal dose, our plate lowers mean W1 against untrained coding and the tested Fisher and coherence criteria across four reconstruction methods. With TV, mean W1 falls from $0.371\um$ for untrained coding to $0.218\um$ for ours; the Fisher design gives $0.251\um$ at similar PSNR to ours. Under iterative reconstruction, mean W1 differs from the end-to-end plate's by less than $0.01\um$, while end-to-end remains better with its own decoder. Crossing separation improves over untrained coding under two of three iterative methods. Together, these results support optical-model-only illumination learning and the value of evaluating depth profiles and filament separation beyond intensity similarity.
